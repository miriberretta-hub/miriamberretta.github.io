About

I study the causal effects of shocks — infrastructure projects, natural disasters, armed conflict — on intimate partner violence and other forms of gender-based violence, using difference-in-differences and event-study designs on administrative health, crime, and employment records.

Before starting my PhD, I spent five years as a Senior Research Associate at 3ie (International Initiative for Impact Evaluation), where I worked on systematic reviews and evidence gap maps. I hold an MSc in Development Economics from the University of East Anglia.

I work in English and Italian, and communicate professionally in Spanish.

Infrastructure and Intimate Partner Violence: Evidence from the Tren Maya

with Adán Silveiro Murillo (Tecnológico de Monterrey)

Using staggered difference-in-differences on hospital injury, crime, and formal employment records across 43 treated municipalities in the Yucatán Peninsula, this paper identifies the effect of large infrastructure construction on intimate partner violence and non-partner female assault.
